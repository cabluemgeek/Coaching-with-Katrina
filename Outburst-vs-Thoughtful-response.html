When an Outburst Wants to Take Over and How to Choose Something Better

There is a moment in every hard conversation when you feel it. The sudden spike in your chest, the warmth behind your face, the tightening in your jaw, your body’s old survival instincts kicking in. This is often where an outburst begins.

I know this because I have lived it for so many years. As a child, I had bully brothers, physically stronger, and I tried every tactic I could to cope. I complained to my parents and felt a small satisfaction when my brothers were scolded. Yet deep down, I never fought back as a kid. I just cried, moved on, and held the hurt inside.

Into my twenties, I faced friends and communities who broke my heart, lied, and tried to destroy parts of my life. I did not fight back - I only wept and pretended to be strong on the surface while I crumbled inside.

Then, at around 25, I started reacting, standing up for those who were unfairly treated, anyone bullied or harmed. I became the person who fought back, who would not allow anyone to be trampled upon. I thought it was the only way to heal those old wounds.

But I learned the hard way that constant fighting does not bring peace. It made me ill and created problems in my body and spirit. I share this now, so you know you are not alone, and so you do not have to go through that same cycle.

A pause is a choice, not a weakness

These little practices, naming the spike, buying a few seconds, checking your inner story, are the pauses that allow you to respond from who you truly are, not just from old survival instincts.

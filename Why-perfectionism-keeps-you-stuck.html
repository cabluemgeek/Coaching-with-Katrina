Why perfectionism keeps you stuck

It was never about getting it right — it's about staying in motion.

← Back to Blog
Mindset 4 min read

Perfectionism doesn't feel like a flaw from the inside. It feels like care, like standing guard over your own standards. But underneath it is usually something quieter: a fear that if the work isn't perfect, it isn't safe to show — to a boss, a partner, or even yourself.

The problem isn't wanting things to be good. It's that perfectionism quietly redefines "good" as "finished being afraid" — a bar that keeps moving every time you get close to it. You finish the project, and instead of relief, you find three more things to fix.

The cost isn't the work — it's the waiting

Most of my clients don't struggle with quality. They struggle with starting, or with shipping. The emails that sit as drafts for weeks. The application that's "almost ready" for months. Perfectionism rarely shows up as bad work — it shows up as delayed work, or work that never leaves the drawer at all.

Underneath that delay is usually a quiet trade: staying in "almost done" feels safer than being seen and judged. As long as it's not finished, it can't be rejected. But that safety has a cost — it's also the reason nothing moves.

"It was never about perfection."

The way out isn't lowering your standards — it's separating "good work" from "safe to be seen." That's usually where the real shift happens: not in the quality of what you make, but in your relationship to being seen making it.

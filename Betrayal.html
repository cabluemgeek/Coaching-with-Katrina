Betrayal

Because the moment you choose yourself, the betrayal loses its final power over you.

← Back to Blog
Betrayal 10 min read

There is a particular kind of pain that comes with betrayal.

It is not simply sadness.

It is not simply anger.

It is the kind of pain that can make you feel as though something inside you has been torn open and left exposed to the world.

Your heart is broken.

Your energy is depleted.

You are exhausted from trying to understand something that makes no sense.

You have cried so much that you wonder whether there are any tears left inside you.

And yet, somehow, they still come.

You cry in the morning.

You cry in the shower.

You cry in the car.

You cry when everyone is asleep.

You cry when you are alone.

Sometimes you do not even know why you are crying anymore.

You have simply reached that place where the pain has become too large for words.

Perhaps you have been crying every day for weeks.

Perhaps you have barely slept.

Perhaps you wake up and, for one brief and merciful second, you forget.

And then you remember.

And the pain comes rushing back.

Your body feels heavy.

Your mind will not stop.

You replay conversations.

You remember things they said.

You remember things they did.

You search for the moment when everything changed.

You wonder what was real.

You wonder what was a lie.

You wonder whether the person you loved ever truly existed in the way you believed they did.

And you are tired.

So very tired.

You are living in survival mode.

You go on holiday, hoping the change of scenery will save you for a few days, but you cannot enjoy it.

You sit in a beautiful place, surrounded by beautiful things, and your mind is somewhere else.

You go to a restaurant and the food has no taste.

You meet your friends and you try to smile, but somewhere inside you are drowning.

They are talking.

They are laughing.

Life is continuing around you.

And yet you are not really there.

Because your mind keeps returning to the same place.

To them.

To what they did.

To what they said.

To what they hid.

To what you discovered.

To everything you now wish you had known sooner.

You can be surrounded by people and still feel completely alone.

You can be in the most beautiful place in the world and feel as though you are sitting in darkness.

You can have everything around you and still feel as though something essential has been taken from you.

That is what betrayal can do.

It can consume you.

It can enter your thoughts before you have even opened your eyes in the morning.

It can follow you into the shower.

It can sit beside you at the dinner table.

It can lie beside you in bed.

It can make you question your memories, your instincts, your judgement and sometimes even your worth.

And if this is where you are right now, before anything else, I want you to know this:

I see you.

I know this hurts.

And I am not going to ask you to pretend that you are fine.

You are not fine.

You have been wounded.

Something you trusted has been broken.

Something you believed was safe has suddenly become a source of pain.

And you are allowed to grieve that.

You are allowed to be angry.

You are allowed to be confused.

You are allowed to miss someone who hurt you.

You are allowed to love someone and still know that you cannot remain where you are being hurt.

You are allowed to have days when you feel strong and days when you feel as though you have fallen apart all over again.

Please do not judge yourself for any of it.

Please do not look at yourself in this moment and decide that this broken, exhausted, frightened version of you is who you are.

It is not who you are.

It is where you are.

And there is a profound difference.

You are not your anxiety.

You are not your sleepless nights.

You are not the tears that will not stop.

You are not the racing thoughts.

You are not the emptiness you feel when you wake up.

You are not the fear.

You are not the anger.

You are not the person betrayal has temporarily made you feel like.

You are the person underneath all of it.

And that person is still there.

Perhaps very quietly.

Perhaps buried beneath the grief.

Perhaps exhausted from surviving.

But still there.

And when the time comes, even if you cannot feel it yet, you will begin to find your way back to that person.

But first, you have to meet yourself exactly where you are.

Not where you think you should be.

Not where everyone else thinks you should be.

Not where you wish you were.

Where you actually are.

Sit with that person.

The one who is tired.

The one who is hurting.

The one who has cried until there seemed to be nothing left to cry.

Do not rush them.

Do not shame them.

Do not tell them to get over it.

Do not tell them they should have known better.

Just sit beside them.

And say:

I know.

I know you are tired.

I know you did not deserve this.

I know you do not understand.

I know part of you wishes you could go back and change everything.

I know you are frightened about what happens next.

But I am here now.

And I am not leaving you.

Perhaps that is where healing begins.

Not with forgiveness.

Not with forgetting.

Not with closure.

But with compassion.

With the willingness to stop fighting yourself for being hurt.

With the willingness to place a hand over your own heart and acknowledge:

Something happened to me, and it hurt me deeply.

And once you can do that, something begins to change.

Not immediately.

Not dramatically.

But quietly.

You begin to awaken.

You begin to realise that although you cannot change what happened, you can choose what happens next.

And that is when the questions slowly begin to change.

In the beginning, we ask:

How could he betray me?

How could she do this to me?

How could someone who said they loved me lie to me?

How could they look me in the eyes and continue?

And those questions are understandable.

You are human.

You are hurt.

You are angry.

You are trying to make sense of something that shattered the reality you believed you were living in.

But eventually, if we are brave enough, we have to begin asking different questions.

Not because what they did was our fault.

It was not.

Another person's dishonesty is not something we caused by loving them.

Another person's cruelty is not something we deserved because we trusted them.

Another person's betrayal belongs to them.

But healing eventually asks something different of us.

It asks us to turn around.

To stop staring at the door through which they left and look at the person standing alone in the room.

Us.

And that is where the real work begins.

Because there is something we must learn about betrayal, perhaps one of the hardest truths we will ever have to accept:

Nobody owes us closure.

Not the person who hurt us.

Not the person who lied to us.

Not the person who betrayed our trust.

Not the person who gaslit us.

Not the person who left us questioning everything we thought was real.

We desperately want closure from the person who broke us.

We want the confession.

The explanation.

The apology.

The moment when they finally look at us and understand what they have done.

We want them to carry some of the weight.

We want them to tell us that it was not our imagination.

That it happened.

That it mattered.

That we mattered.

But what if they never do?

What if the apology never comes?

What if the explanation is another lie?

What if they never become the person capable of giving us the ending we need?

Then what?

Do we spend the rest of our lives standing at the door, waiting for someone who wounded us to return with the medicine?

There is a terrible freedom in understanding that they may never come.

Nobody owes us closure.

And the moment we stop waiting for the person who hurt us to heal us, something begins to loosen inside us.

We take back the responsibility for our own healing.

Not because we caused the wound.

But because we deserve to decide what happens to us after it.

This is where betrayal becomes something more than a story about what someone did to us.

It becomes a story about whether we will continue abandoning ourselves because someone else did.

And that is the question we do not like to ask.

Who is betraying whom now?

They betrayed your trust.

But are you betraying yourself every morning you wake up and hand them another piece of your peace?

Are you betraying yourself every night you replay their words until you can no longer hear your own?

Are you betraying yourself when you allow their choices to determine how worthy you feel?

When you allow their lies to become the truth you tell yourself?

When you allow what they did to become the measure of what you believe you deserve?

These questions are painful.

But they are not accusations.

They are invitations.

Invitations to come back to yourself.

Because betrayal has a strange way of making us abandon the person who needs us most.

Ourselves.

We become so consumed by what happened that we forget to ask what is happening inside us now.

We forget to eat.

We forget to rest.

We stop listening to our bodies.

We live inside our heads.

We search for answers in old conversations.

We rehearse arguments that will never happen.

We imagine the words we wish we had said.

We imagine the words they should have said.

We create entire conversations with someone who may never understand us.

And while we are busy trying to make them understand our pain, our own soul is quietly asking:

When will you come back for me?

That is the question healing begins with.

Not:

Why did they do this?

But:

What do I need now?

What is my heart trying to tell me?

What did I ignore?

Where did I silence myself?

Where did I make excuses for what was hurting me?

Where did I confuse patience with self abandonment?

Where did I keep giving chances because I was afraid of what would happen if I finally chose myself?

And we must ask these questions gently.

Without hatred.

Without shame.

Without turning the knife towards ourselves.

Because we have already suffered enough.

We do not need to become another person who hurts us.

We need to become the person who finally stands beside us.

The person who finally says:

I believe you.

I see what happened.

You did not deserve to be treated this way.

And I am not abandoning you now.

Perhaps we spend too much of our lives waiting for other people to love us enough to protect us.

We want someone to see our wounds before we speak of them.

We want someone to notice when we are disappearing.

We want someone to stand between us and the things that hurt us.

But there is a kind of love we eventually have to learn that no one else can give us.

The love that says:

I will not leave myself here.

I will listen when something feels wrong.

I will honour the voice I kept silencing.

I will stop explaining away what repeatedly hurts me.

I will stop begging for the bare minimum and calling it love.

I will stop confusing someone's potential with their reality.

I will stop asking another person to give me the safety I refuse to give myself.

And slowly, painfully, imperfectly, we begin to rebuild.

Not overnight.

Never overnight.

Healing does not happen because one morning you decide you are finished being hurt.

The heart does not understand deadlines.

The body does not forget because the mind has decided to move on.

There will be days when you feel free.

And then there will be a song.

A photograph.

A familiar street.

A scent.

A name.

A dream.

And suddenly, without warning, you are back there.

Back in that room.

Back in that moment.

Back inside the version of yourself who did not yet know what was coming.

Do not hate yourself for going back.

Sometimes healing means visiting the same wound many times until it no longer has the power to frighten you.

There will be progress.

There will be setbacks.

There will be tears you thought you had already cried.

There will be anger you thought you had already released.

Let them come.

Let them pass.

Be patient with yourself.

Breathe.

Rest.

Regulate your nervous system.

Seek support.

Let your body learn that the danger has passed.

Let your heart learn that not everyone who enters your life is entitled to remain there.

Let yourself grieve the person you thought they were.

Sometimes that is the deepest grief of all.

You are not only mourning what they did.

You are mourning the future you imagined with someone who never intended to meet you there.

You are mourning conversations that will never happen.

Apologies that may never come.

Promises that turned out to be empty.

You are mourning the version of yourself who believed the story would end differently.

And that grief deserves tenderness.

Then, eventually, comes forgiveness.

But forgiveness is often misunderstood.

Forgiveness does not mean saying:

What you did was acceptable.

It does not mean:

You may come back.

It does not mean:

I trust you again.

It does not mean:

I have forgotten.

Sometimes forgiveness has nothing to do with the person who hurt you.

Sometimes forgiveness is looking at yourself and whispering:

I forgive you.

I forgive you for what you did not know.

I forgive you for what you could not see.

I forgive you for staying.

I forgive you for hoping.

I forgive you for believing.

I forgive you for the boundaries you did not yet know how to hold.

I forgive you for not recognising the warning signs.

I forgive you for being human.

And perhaps that is the forgiveness we need most.

Because there is a terrible weight in spending years punishing yourself for the moment you failed to protect yourself.

You cannot go back.

You cannot become the person you needed then.

But you can become that person now.

You can become the one who notices.

The one who listens.

The one who leaves.

The one who says no.

The one who does not have to be destroyed before they decide they have had enough.

And one day, something beautiful happens.

You stop checking whether they are sorry.

You stop wondering whether they understand.

You stop imagining the conversation.

You stop needing them to admit what they did.

Their name becomes only a name.

The memory becomes only a memory.

And the story that once consumed you becomes a chapter you can finally close without needing them to hold the pen.

That is freedom.

Because the person who betrayed you may have taken something from you once.

But they do not have to keep taking it.

They do not get your mornings.

They do not get your sleep.

They do not get your future.

They do not get to live inside every beautiful thing that happens to you.

They do not get to become the reason you stop dreaming.

They do not get to turn you into someone who no longer believes in love.

Do not give them that much of you.

Your life is too sacred.

Your energy is too precious.

There are still dreams inside you that have never had the chance to breathe.

There are mornings you have not yet woken up to with peace in your chest.

There are people you have not yet met who will love you without asking you to betray yourself to keep them.

There are places you have not yet seen.

There is laughter waiting for you.

There is a version of you waiting on the other side of this pain.

Do not let someone else's brokenness become the architecture of your life.

What they did is part of your story.

It is not the whole story.

Their choice was theirs.

Their behaviour was theirs.

Their betrayal was theirs.

But what you choose to do with the life that remains in your hands is yours.

You are not responsible for making sure they never hurt another person.

You are responsible for learning how to protect yourself.

You are responsible for rebuilding your trust in your own instincts.

You are responsible for becoming faithful to yourself.

And perhaps this is the deepest lesson betrayal leaves behind.

Not that you should never trust again.

Not that you should become cold.

Not that you should build walls so high that nothing can ever reach you.

But that you must learn the difference between having an open heart and leaving your heart unguarded.

You can remain soft without remaining unprotected.

You can forgive without returning.

You can love without losing yourself.

You can remember without remaining there.

And you can grieve someone who hurt you while still choosing to save yourself.

One day, you will look back at the person you were when all of this happened.

You will remember how desperately they wanted answers.

How badly they wanted to be understood.

How they lay awake at night trying to solve a mystery they did not create.

You will remember how much they cried.

How much they questioned themselves.

How often they wondered whether they were enough.

And you will want to reach through time and hold that person.

You will want to tell them:

It was never because you were not enough.

You were simply loving someone who did not know how to honour what they had been given.

And then you will realise something.

You survived.

Not because they apologised.

Not because they changed.

Not because they gave you closure.

You survived because, somewhere beneath all that pain, there was still a small part of you whispering:

Come back.

Come back to your life.

Come back to your body.

Come back to your dreams.

Come back to your laughter.

Come back to your dignity.

Come back to yourself.

And perhaps that is what healing really is.

Not forgetting what happened.

Not pretending it did not hurt.

Not becoming indifferent.

But slowly, quietly, tenderly becoming yours again.

Until one day you realise that the person who betrayed you is no longer the centre of the story.

You are.

And this time, you are not waiting for someone else to choose you.

You have chosen yourself.

You are not waiting for someone else to protect you.

You have learnt to protect yourself.

You are not waiting for someone else to give you closure.

You have given yourself permission to close the door.

And you do not close it because you stopped caring.

You close it because you finally understood that your peace is worth more than an explanation.

That your future is worth more than their apology.

That your life is worth more than the pain they left behind.

And perhaps the final act of healing is the quietest one of all.

You stop asking:

Why did they betray me?

And you begin asking:

Why would I betray myself by staying there any longer?

That question changes everything.

Because the moment you choose yourself, the betrayal loses its final power over you.

And one day, when you think of it, you will not feel the same ache.

You will simply remember.

And then you will breathe.

And you will keep walking.

Not because it never happened.

But because it no longer owns you.

You found your way back.

And this time,

you came home to yourself.

Values

Our values are more about what we do, rather than what we believe in.

← Back to Blog
Values 5 min read

We often think our values are the things we believe.

Or the things we say we believe.

But perhaps our values are something much simpler and much harder to hide.

Our values are what we do.

They show up in the way we speak when we are frustrated.

In how we bahave when things do not go our way.

In how we treat someone who disagrees with us.

In how we respond when someone does not listen to us

In the patience we have with people who cannot do anything for us.

Values are not simply words we use to describe ourselves. They are reflected in our everyday choices.

If we say we value our health, we show it through our actions. We make time to exercise. We go to the gym consistently. We pay attention to what we eat. We rest when our body needs it. We take care of ourselves even when it is inconvenient. The consistency of those actions is what demonstrates that our health truly matters to us.

In the same way, values are revealed in how a husband and wife treat each other, especially when they are angry or disappointed.

They are revealed in how a mother and father speak to their child when the child makes a mistake or does not behave as expected.

They are revealed in how we treat our friends when they disagree with us and how we treat our colleagues when we are under pressure.

And they are revealed in how we speak to people who serve us or help us, whether it is a server at a restaurant, a driver, a cleaner, or someone who works in our home.

It is easy to say, "I value respect."

It is much harder to remain respectful when we are irritated.

It is easy to say, "I believe in kindness."

It is much harder to be kind when we feel we have been wronged.

Sometimes we excuse our behaviour by saying, “That is just my habit,” or “That is just the way I am.” Perhaps it is a habit. Perhaps it is the way we have learnt to respond. But recognising that is not the same as accepting that it should remain that way.

We all lose our patience. We all have moments we are not proud of. Having good values does not mean being perfect.

It means that when our actions do not match the values we claim to have, we are willing to notice it, take responsibility, and change.

That willingness, that courage to change, is where our true values live.

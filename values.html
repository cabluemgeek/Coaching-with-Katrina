Values

Our values are more about what we do, rather than what we believe in.

← Back to Blog
Values 5 min read

We often imagine our values are the things we believe. Or the things we say we believe. But perhaps values are far more honest than that. Perhaps they are the things we cannot hide, even when we try.

Our values are revealed in what we do. In the tone we use when frustration rises. In the choices we make when life does not bend to our will. In how we treat someone who challenges us. In how we respond when we feel unheard. In the patience we offer to people who can do nothing for us.

Values are not the words we choose to descrube ourselves. They are the quiet evidence of our everyday behaviour.

If we say we value our health, it shows in the way we live. We move our bodies. We nourish ourselves. We rest when we are tired. We honour our wellbeing even when it is inconvinient. Consistency becomes the proof that our health truly matters.

The same is true in our relationships. Values appear in how a husband and wife speak to each other when they are hurt. In how a mother or father responds when their child makes a mistake. In how we treat our friends when they disagree with us. In how we treat our colleagues when pressure is high. And in how we speak to the people who serve us, the waiter, the driver, the cleaner, the person who shows up quietly to help.

It is easy to say, "I value respect."

It is far harder to remain respectful when irritation takes over. It is easy to say, "I believe in kindness.". It is far harder to be kind when we feel wronged.

We often excuse ourselves with, "That's just my habit," or "That's just how I am.". Perhaps it is a habit. Perhaps it is how we learnt to survive. But recognising it is not the same as accepting tat it must stay that way.

We all lose our patience. We all have moments we wish we could take back. Having good values does not mean being perfect.

It means that when our actions fall short of the values we claim to hold, we are willing to notice it, take responsibility, and choose differently.

That willingness, that courage to change, is where our true values live.
